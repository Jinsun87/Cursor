## Settling in

Let yourself rest now.

Breathe in slowly, and let it out slowly. Feel the stillness of the room around you.

Let your thoughts slow down, like footsteps slowing at the end of a long walk.

[pause]

Tonight's story ends in a garden, very early in the morning, while it is still dark. It's about a woman who loved Jesus, who stayed when others ran, and who was the first person ever to see Him alive again. Her name was Mary Magdalene.

Just let the story come.

[pause]

## Part one: Mary of Magdala

On the western shore of the Sea of Galilee there was a fishing town called Magdala. That is where Mary came from, and so people called her Mary Magdalene, to tell her apart from the other Marys of her time.

We know very little about her early life. Luke tells us just one thing. Jesus had set her free from seven demons. Whatever she had suffered, it had been deep, and it had been long. And Jesus had made her whole.

[pause]

Over the centuries, some people came to think of Mary Magdalene as a woman with a sinful past, mixing her up with other women in the Gospels. But the Bible never says that about her. It says only that she had been healed, and that she followed Him.

[long pause]

## Part two: following

After she was healed, Mary didn't go back to her old life in Magdala. She followed Jesus.

Luke tells us that as Jesus travelled from town to town, preaching the good news of the kingdom of God, the twelve disciples were with Him. And so were some women who had been healed by Him: Mary called Magdalene, and Joanna, whose husband managed King Herod's household, and Susanna, and many others.

> These women were ministering to them out of their own means. [Luke 8:3]

[pause]

Think about what that means. Jesus and His disciples had no home and no regular income. These women paid for the bread, found places to stay, carried what was needed, and cared for them, day after day, mile after mile. Without them, the journeys of Jesus might hardly have been possible.

Mary Magdalene was among them, through Galilee, and all the way to Jerusalem.

[long pause]

## Part three: she stayed

Then came the darkest days.

In Jerusalem, Jesus was arrested, and condemned, and crucified. Most of His disciples ran away and hid.

But not all of them. Mark tells us there were women there, watching from a distance. Among them was Mary Magdalene. John tells us that at one point she was standing near the cross, with Jesus' mother.

We won't stay there long tonight. But notice simply this. When it would have been easier, and safer, to be anywhere else, Mary stayed. She did not leave Him.

[pause]

Late that afternoon, two men who had followed Jesus quietly came forward. One was Joseph of Arimathea, a disciple who had kept his faith secret out of fear. He went to the governor, Pilate, and asked for the body of Jesus, and Pilate let him take it. The other was Nicodemus, the man who had once come to Jesus at night with his questions. He brought a great weight of myrrh and aloes.

Together they wrapped the body of Jesus in linen cloths, with the spices, as was the custom. Near the place where He had died there was a garden, and in the garden a new tomb, where no one had ever been laid. Because the Sabbath was about to begin, and the tomb was close by, they laid Him there.

And Mary Magdalene was there, watching. She saw where He was laid.

[pause]

Then the Sabbath began, and she rested, as the law commanded. It must have been the longest day of her life. A whole day of stillness, with nothing to do but grieve. The women had prepared spices and perfumes, ready to go back as soon as the Sabbath was over, and care for His body properly. It was the last thing they could do for Him.

[long pause]

## Part four: while it was still dark

On the first day of the week, very early in the morning, while it was still dark, Mary Magdalene went to the tomb.

Picture the streets of Jerusalem before dawn. Cold, grey, silent. Mary walking with her grief through the empty city.

And when she reached the tomb, she saw that the great stone had been rolled away from the entrance.

[pause]

She ran. She ran to Simon Peter and to the other disciple, the one Jesus loved, and she said:

> They have taken the Lord out of the tomb… and we do not know where they have put Him! [John 20:2]

The two men ran to the tomb. The younger one got there first, and looked in, and saw the linen cloths lying there. Then Peter arrived and went straight in. He saw the linen cloths, and the cloth that had been around Jesus' head, rolled up and lying by itself.

Then the other disciple went in too. And he saw, and believed. They still didn't understand what had happened. But they went back home.

[long pause]

## Part five: Mary in the garden

Mary didn't go home.

> But Mary stood outside the tomb weeping. [John 20:11]

As she wept, she bent down to look into the tomb. And she saw two angels in white, sitting where the body of Jesus had been, one at the head and one at the feet.

They said to her, “Woman, why are you weeping?” [John 20:13]

And she said, “Because they have taken my Lord away… and I do not know where they have put Him.” [John 20:13]

[pause]

Even angels couldn't comfort her. She only wanted Him.

[pause]

When she had said this, she turned around, and saw someone standing there. It was Jesus. But she didn't recognize Him.

> Woman, why are you weeping?… Whom are you seeking? [John 20:15]

She thought He was the gardener. And she said, “Sir, if you have carried Him off, tell me where you have put Him, and I will get Him.” [John 20:15]

[long pause]

And then Jesus said one word.

> Mary. [John 20:16]

[long pause]

Just her name. Spoken the way only He had ever said it.

And she turned, and said to Him in her own language, “Rabboni!” [John 20:16]

Which means, Teacher.

[long pause]

## Part six: I have seen the Lord

She must have reached out to hold on to Him. And Jesus said, “Do not cling to Me,” [John 20:17] because He had not yet gone up to the Father. Instead, He gave her something to do.

> But go and tell My brothers, ‘I am ascending to My Father and your Father, to My God and your God.’ [John 20:17]

My Father, and your Father. My God, and your God.

Think of what that meant to her. Three days before, she had watched Him die, and thought everything was lost. Now He was calling His frightened, scattered friends His brothers, and telling them that His Father was their Father too. Nothing had been lost after all. Everything had been given.

[pause]

So Mary Magdalene went to the disciples, and she said the words that Christians have repeated ever since:

> I have seen the Lord! [John 20:18]

And she told them everything He had said to her.

[pause]

Luke tells the morning from the side of the women together, Mary Magdalene among them, with Joanna and Mary the mother of James and others. They came to the tomb at early dawn with their spices, and found the stone rolled away, and the body gone. As they stood there not knowing what to think, two men in shining clothes stood beside them. And the women bowed their faces to the ground, afraid. And the men said:

> Why do you look for the living among the dead? [Luke 24:5]

> He is not here; He has risen! [Luke 24:6]

They reminded the women of what Jesus Himself had told them back in Galilee: that He would be handed over, and crucified, and on the third day rise again. And the women remembered His words.

[pause]

When the women told the apostles, at first their words seemed like nonsense, and they didn't believe them. But it was true. And the one Jesus chose to tell first, the first witness of the resurrection, was Mary of Magdala, the woman who had stayed.

[long pause]

## What Scripture remembers

All four Gospels place Mary Magdalene at the empty tomb that first morning. Her name stands first, again and again, in the lists of the women who followed Jesus and who saw the empty tomb.

Some early Christian writers gave her a title: the apostle to the apostles. Because she was the one sent to tell them.

[pause]

## Closing

Mary Magdalene's story begins with being set free, and it ends with being sent. In between, she simply stayed close to Jesus. When it was hard, when it was dangerous, when it seemed to be over, she stayed.

And because she stayed, she was there when He called her by her name.

[pause]

Whatever you are carrying tonight, whatever grief or worry, He knows your name too. And He says it the same way He said hers, in the garden, in the early morning.

[long pause]

Let your breathing slow now.

Picture that garden in the first grey light. The dew on the grass. The stone rolled away. The empty tomb behind, and the first birds beginning to sing. The darkness ending.

[pause]

Rest now. Morning is coming.

Goodnight.

[long pause]

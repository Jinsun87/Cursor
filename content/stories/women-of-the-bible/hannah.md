## Settling in

Let the day go now.

Let your hands rest, and your jaw soften, and your breathing find its own slow pace. In. And out.

If your mind is still busy, that's all right. You don't need to quiet it by force. Just notice the thoughts, the way you might notice people passing on a road, and let them go on their way.

Breathe in once more. And let it go.

[pause]

Tonight's story is about a woman who prayed so quietly that no one could hear her, and so honestly that she poured out everything she had. Her name was Hannah.

There's nothing to hold on to. Just listen, and let yourself drift.

[pause]

## Part one: year after year

In the hill country of Ephraim there lived a man named Elkanah. He had two wives. One was named Hannah, and the other Peninnah. Peninnah had children. But Hannah had none.

Every year, Elkanah took his whole family up to Shiloh, to worship the LORD and offer sacrifice at the house of the LORD there. Shiloh was where the tabernacle stood in those days, the tent of meeting from the time of Moses, with the ark of the covenant inside. It was a journey up through the hills, and the whole household made it together, with animals for the offering, and Peninnah's children on the road beside them. And when the day came for the sacrifice, he gave portions of the meat to Peninnah and to all her sons and daughters.

But to Hannah, he gave a double portion. Because he loved her.

[pause]

That should have been comfort enough. But it wasn't. Peninnah was her rival, and she knew exactly where it hurt. Year after year, when they went up to the house of the LORD, she taunted Hannah about having no children, until Hannah wept and could not eat.

Elkanah tried to comfort her. He said:

> Hannah, why are you crying? [1 Samuel 1:8]

> Why won't you eat? Why is your heart so grieved? Am I not better to you than ten sons? [1 Samuel 1:8]

He meant it kindly. But some sorrows can't be argued away, even by someone who loves you.

[long pause]

## Part two: a prayer without a voice

One year at Shiloh, after the meal was over, Hannah got up from the table. Eli the priest was sitting on a chair by the doorpost of the house of the LORD.

And Hannah, in her bitter distress, prayed to the LORD and wept with many tears.

She made a promise:

> O LORD of Hosts, if only You will look upon the affliction of Your maidservant and remember me, not forgetting Your maidservant but giving her a son, then I will dedicate him to the LORD all the days of his life [1 Samuel 1:11]

[pause]

She prayed for a long time. Her lips were moving, but she was praying in her heart, and no sound came out.

Eli was watching her. And he thought she was drunk. He said to her, “How long will you be drunk? Put away your wine!” [1 Samuel 1:14]

Imagine being misunderstood like that, at the lowest moment of your life, by the very person who should have understood.

But Hannah answered him gently and honestly:

> No, my lord… I am a woman troubled in spirit. I have not had any wine or strong drink, but I have poured out my soul before the LORD. [1 Samuel 1:15]

> Do not take your servant for a wicked woman, for all this time I have been praying out of the depth of my anguish and grief. [1 Samuel 1:16]

She didn't defend herself angrily. She didn't pretend to be fine. She simply told the truth: I am troubled, and I have been praying.

[pause]

Poured out my soul. That is what prayer was for Hannah. Not fine words. Not a performance. Just everything, emptied out before God, the way you might tip out a jar until nothing is left.

Eli understood then. And he said to her:

> Go in peace… and may the God of Israel grant the petition you have asked of Him. [1 Samuel 1:17]

And Hannah said, “May your maidservant find favor with you.” [1 Samuel 1:18]

[pause]

Hannah went on her way. And the Bible says something very simple and very lovely about her. She began to eat again, and her face was no longer downcast.

Nothing had changed yet. There was no child. No sign. But she had given her burden to God, and she had left it with Him.

[long pause]

## Part three: asked of the LORD

Early the next morning, the family worshipped before the LORD, and then went home to Ramah.

And the Bible says the LORD remembered Hannah.

In the course of time, she gave birth to a son. And she named him Samuel, saying:

> Because I have asked for him from the LORD. [1 Samuel 1:20]

[pause]

When the next year came, and the family went up to Shiloh again, Hannah stayed at home. She told her husband that she would wait until the boy was weaned, and then take him to the house of the LORD, to stay there for good.

Elkanah said, “Do what you think is best,” [1 Samuel 1:23] and he added a prayer of his own: “Only may the LORD confirm His word.” [1 Samuel 1:23]

So Hannah stayed home and nursed her son. Those were her years with him: the small hands, the first words, the nights rocking him to sleep. She knew they would not last for ever. And she had promised.

[pause]

## Part four: given back

When he was weaned, Hannah took him up to Shiloh. She brought a generous offering with her: a three-year-old bull, a large measure of flour, and a skin of wine. The road climbed up through the hills, the same road she had walked so many times in tears. This time she walked it with her son beside her, though he was still young.

At the house of the LORD they offered the sacrifice. Then they brought the boy to Eli, the same old priest who had once mistaken her prayers for drunkenness. And Hannah said:

> Please, my lord… as surely as you live, my lord, I am the woman who stood here beside you praying to the LORD. [1 Samuel 1:26]

> I prayed for this boy, and since the LORD has granted me what I asked of Him, I now dedicate the boy to the LORD. [1 Samuel 1:27–28]

And they worshipped the LORD there.

[pause]

It is hard to imagine what that cost her. The child she had wept for, year after year, she now placed into God's hands. Not because she loved him less, but because she trusted God more.

[long pause]

## Part five: Hannah's song

And then Hannah prayed again. But this time it wasn't a silent prayer. It was a song.

> My heart rejoices in the LORD; my horn is exalted in the LORD. [1 Samuel 2:1]

> There is no one holy like the LORD. Indeed, there is no one besides You! And there is no Rock like our God. [1 Samuel 2:2]

She sang about a God who turns things upside down. The proud are told to stop their boasting, because the LORD is a God who knows, and He weighs what people do.

> The bows of the mighty are broken, but the feeble are equipped with strength. [1 Samuel 2:4]

> The well-fed hire themselves out for food, but the starving hunger no more. The barren woman gives birth to seven [1 Samuel 2:5]

The barren woman. She was singing about herself, though she put it in the biggest terms she could find, as if her one answered prayer were a window onto the way God treats the whole world.

> He raises the poor from the dust and lifts the needy from the ash heap. [1 Samuel 2:8]

> He guards the steps of His faithful ones [1 Samuel 2:9]

[pause]

A woman who had been taunted, overlooked and misunderstood, singing that God lifts the needy from the ash heap. She knew it was true. It had happened to her.

[pause]

## Part six: the little robe

Then the family went home to Ramah. And the boy Samuel stayed in Shiloh, serving the LORD under Eli the priest, wearing a small linen ephod, a simple garment for those who served.

And every year, the Bible tells us:

> his mother would make him a little robe and bring it to him when she went with her husband to offer the annual sacrifice. [1 Samuel 2:19]

[pause]

Think of her sewing it. Through the long months at home, measuring with her eyes how much he might have grown since last year. A little bigger each time. And each year, carrying it up the road to Shiloh to put it in his hands.

[pause]

Eli blessed Elkanah and Hannah. He prayed that the LORD would give them more children, in place of the one she had given.

And the LORD was kind to Hannah. She had three more sons and two daughters.

Meanwhile, the boy Samuel grew up in the presence of the LORD.

> And the boy Samuel continued to grow in stature and in favor with the LORD and with man. [1 Samuel 2:26]

[long pause]

## What Scripture remembers

Samuel grew up to be one of the great prophets of Israel. The LORD was with him, and all Israel knew it.

And more than a thousand years later, a young woman named Mary, expecting a child she had not asked for, sang a song that sounds very much like Hannah's:

> He has brought down rulers from their thrones, but has exalted the humble. [Luke 1:52]

> He has filled the hungry with good things [Luke 1:53]

Hannah's song was still being sung.

[pause]

## Closing

Hannah's story isn't really about getting what she wanted. It's about where she took her sorrow. Not into bitterness. Not into silence. She took it to God, all of it, and poured it out.

And when she got up from that prayer, before anything had changed, she ate, and her face was lifted.

[pause]

If there's something heavy on your heart tonight, you can do what Hannah did. You don't need the right words. You don't even need to say them out loud. You can simply pour it out, and leave it there.

[long pause]

Let your breathing slow now.

Picture the lamps being lit at Shiloh in the evening, and a small boy asleep in the house of the LORD, with a new robe folded beside him, made by his mother's hands.

[pause]

You are not overlooked. You are heard.

Rest now.

Goodnight.

[long pause]

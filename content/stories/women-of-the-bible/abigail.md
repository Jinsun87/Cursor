## Settling in

Let yourself sink a little deeper into the bed.

Breathe in slowly. Hold it for a moment. And let it go.

Let your forehead smooth out. Let your shoulders fall. Whatever happened today, whatever was said or left unsaid, you can set it down for now.

[pause]

Tonight's story is about a quarrel that almost turned into something terrible, and a woman who stepped into the middle of it. She had no sword and no army. What she had was courage, a quick mind, and the right words at the right moment. Her name was Abigail.

Just listen, and let the story carry you.

[pause]

## Part one: two men

Our story takes place in the days when David was a young man, but not yet king. Years before, the prophet Samuel had anointed him with oil, the sign that God had chosen him to rule Israel one day. But King Saul still sat on the throne, and Saul was jealous of David and wanted him dead.

So David lived in the wilderness, in the hills and caves of Judah, with a band of about six hundred men. Day after day Saul searched for him. But God kept him safe.

Not long before our story, something remarkable had happened. Saul, hunting for David, had gone alone into a cave beside the sheepfolds, not knowing that David and his men were hiding in the dark at the back of it. David's men whispered that this was his chance. God had put his enemy into his hands.

David crept forward in the dark. But all he did was cut off a corner of Saul's robe. And even that troubled his conscience. He said to his men:

> The LORD forbid that I should do such a thing to my master, the LORD's anointed. [1 Samuel 24:6]

He held his men back, and Saul walked out of the cave unharmed.

[pause]

That was David at his best: patient, leaving judgment to God. Hold on to that picture. Because in tonight's story, he very nearly forgets it.

[pause]

Around that time, news came that the old prophet Samuel had died, the boy Hannah once gave back to God, now grown old and grey. All Israel gathered to mourn him, and they buried him at his home in Ramah. And David moved on, deeper into the wilderness.

[pause]

Not far away, in a town called Maon, there lived a very wealthy man. He had a thousand goats and three thousand sheep, and he kept them at Carmel, a small town nearby in the hill country. His name was Nabal.

The Bible describes Nabal in a few blunt words. He was harsh, and his dealings were evil.

His wife was another person altogether. Her name was Abigail, and the Bible says she was both intelligent and beautiful.

[pause]

It was the season of sheep shearing. In those days it was a time of feasting and plenty, when the wool was cut and sold, and a rich man's table was full.

All that time, David's men had been living out in the same hills as Nabal's shepherds. And they had never taken a single sheep. In fact, they had protected Nabal's flocks.

[long pause]

## Part two: a rude answer

So David sent ten of his young men to Nabal at Carmel, with a friendly message. They were to greet him in David's name, and say:

> Long life to you, and peace to you and to your house and to all that belongs to you. [1 Samuel 25:6]

They were to remind him, politely, that his shepherds had been safe with David's men, and that nothing of theirs had gone missing. And since it was a day of feasting, would Nabal please share whatever he could spare.

The young men delivered the message, word for word. And then they waited.

[pause]

Nabal's answer was cold and insulting:

> Who is David? Who is this son of Jesse? Many servants these days are breaking away from their masters. [1 Samuel 25:10]

He would not share his bread or his water or his meat, he said, with men who came from nobody knew where.

The young men turned around and went back to David, and told him every word.

[pause]

David was furious. He told his men, “Strap on your swords!” [1 Samuel 25:13] About four hundred of them followed him, and two hundred stayed behind with the supplies. And they set out toward Carmel, meaning to make Nabal and his household pay for that insult.

[long pause]

## Part three: a servant's warning

Back at Nabal's house, one of the young shepherds went to find Abigail. He told her what had happened, how David had sent messengers with a greeting, and how Nabal had shouted at them.

And then he said something about David's men that Abigail needed to hear:

> They were a wall around us, both day and night, the whole time we were herding our sheep near them. [1 Samuel 25:16]

He told her trouble was coming, to the master and to the whole household. And he added, honestly, that Nabal was such a scoundrel that nobody could even speak to him.

[pause]

Abigail didn't waste a moment. She didn't argue, or panic, or go to her husband. She hurried.

Picture her moving through the house and storerooms, calling to the servants, the courtyard suddenly full of baskets and the sound of donkeys being led out. She gathered two hundred loaves of bread, two skins of wine, five sheep ready for cooking, roasted grain, a hundred clusters of raisins and two hundred cakes of figs. She loaded it all onto donkeys. She sent her servants on ahead, and said she would follow right behind.

And she did not tell her husband.

[pause]

## Part four: in the ravine

Abigail rode her donkey down into a narrow mountain ravine. And there, coming down the other side toward her, were David and his men.

David was still angry. He had just been saying that all his kindness to Nabal had been for nothing, and that Nabal had repaid good with evil. He had sworn that before morning came, he would see that household destroyed.

[pause]

When Abigail saw David, she got down from her donkey at once, and bowed low to the ground in front of him. She fell at his feet, and she said:

> My lord, may the blame be on me alone, but please let your servant speak to you; hear the words of your servant. [1 Samuel 25:24]

Think about that. She took the blame on herself, though it wasn't hers. And she asked only to be heard.

[pause]

She told David not to pay any attention to Nabal. He lived up to his name, she said, and his name means Fool. She hadn't even seen the young men David had sent.

Then she gave him the food she had brought, for his men. And she asked him to forgive her.

[long pause]

## Part five: the right words

And then Abigail said something more. She spoke to David not about the insult, but about his future.

She reminded him that the LORD would surely make him a lasting house, because he fought the LORD's battles. And she prayed:

> May no evil be found in you as long as you live. [1 Samuel 25:28]

She told him that if anyone chased him to take his life, his life would be held safe by God:

> the life of my lord will be bound securely by the LORD your God in the bundle of the living. [1 Samuel 25:29]

And one day, she said, when the LORD had made him ruler over Israel, he would be glad that he had not stained his hands with needless bloodshed and revenge.

And she ended with a small request of her own:

> And when the LORD has dealt well with my lord, may you remember your servant. [1 Samuel 25:31]

[pause]

That was her real gift to him. Not the bread or the figs. She reminded David of who he was, and who God meant him to be, at the very moment he was about to forget it.

The man in the cave had refused to take revenge on a king. Now, stung by a rude shepherd's master, he had been ready to do far worse. It's often like that. We can be patient in the big trials and then lose ourselves over a small insult. Abigail met him there, on the road, and handed him back his better self.

[pause]

David stopped. And his anger turned to thanks.

> Blessed be the LORD, the God of Israel, who sent you to meet me this day! [1 Samuel 25:32]

> Blessed is your discernment, and blessed are you, because today you kept me from bloodshed and from avenging myself by my own hand. [1 Samuel 25:33]

He accepted what she had brought. And he said to her:

> Go home in peace. See, I have heeded your voice and granted your request. [1 Samuel 25:35]

[long pause]

## Part six: home again

When Abigail got home, Nabal was holding a feast fit for a king. He was in high spirits, and very drunk. So she said nothing to him that night. She waited until morning.

In the morning, when he was sober, she told him everything: how close they had all come, and what she had done. And as he heard it, his heart failed within him, and he became like a stone.

About ten days later, the LORD struck Nabal, and he died.

[pause]

It is a sobering ending for him, and the Bible tells it plainly. The man who would listen to no one had come to the end of his own way.

[pause]

When David heard, he gave thanks that God had kept him from doing evil himself, and had judged the matter instead.

Then David sent his servants to Abigail at Carmel, asking her to become his wife. And Abigail bowed, and said she was ready to serve. She got up on her donkey, with five of her maidservants, and followed David's messengers, and became his wife.

[pause]

## What Scripture remembers

The Bible doesn't add a lesson at the end of Abigail's story. But elsewhere it says, in a single line, what she did:

> A gentle answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger. [Proverbs 15:1]

And Jesus said:

> Blessed are the peacemakers, for they will be called sons of God. [Matthew 5:9]

[pause]

## Closing

Abigail lived with a hard man, in a house where nobody could reason with him. She could have stayed quiet and let the trouble come. Instead, she moved quickly, went out to meet the anger head on, and answered it with humility and kindness.

She didn't make David feel small. She reminded him of who he was meant to be. And because of her, a good man didn't do a terrible thing.

[pause]

Maybe tonight there's a conversation you're dreading, or a quarrel that's still raw. You don't need to solve it now. But you can let Abigail's story stay with you as you fall asleep: a gentle word, spoken at the right moment, can turn a whole day around.

[long pause]

Let your breathing slow now.

Picture a quiet road in the hills of Judah, late in the evening. A donkey walking slowly home. The anger of the day gone. Peace on the road behind and ahead.

[pause]

Rest now.

Goodnight.

[long pause]

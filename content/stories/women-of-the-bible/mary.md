## Settling in

Let your body grow still.

Breathe in slowly. And as you breathe out, let your shoulders sink a little lower.

Once more. In. And all the way out.

[pause]

Tonight's story is one many people know from Christmas. But we're going to hear it slowly, through the eyes of one young woman in a small town in Galilee, who was told something so astonishing that she could hardly take it in. Her name was Mary.

Let the familiar words come to you as if for the first time.

[pause]

## Part one: two old people and a long wait

The story begins not with Mary, but with her relative, an older woman named Elizabeth.

In the days of King Herod, there was a priest named Zechariah, and Elizabeth was his wife. The Bible says they were both righteous before God. But they had no children. Elizabeth was barren, and they were both well on in years.

One day, Zechariah was chosen to go into the temple of the Lord and burn incense, while the people prayed outside. And an angel of the Lord appeared to him, standing beside the altar.

Zechariah was afraid. But the angel said:

> Do not be afraid, Zechariah, because your prayer has been heard. Your wife Elizabeth will bear you a son, and you are to give him the name John. [Luke 1:13]

[pause]

Zechariah couldn't believe it. They were too old, he said. And the angel told him his name was Gabriel, and that he stood in the presence of God. Because Zechariah had not believed, he would be unable to speak until the promise came true.

So Zechariah came out of the temple unable to say a word. And not long after, Elizabeth was expecting a child, just as the angel had said. She stayed quietly at home for five months, and she said:

> The Lord has done this for me. In these days He has shown me favor and taken away my disgrace among the people. [Luke 1:25]

[long pause]

## Part two: Nazareth

In the sixth month, God sent the angel Gabriel again. This time not to the temple in Jerusalem, but to Nazareth, a small town in Galilee. He was sent to a young woman who was pledged to be married to a man named Joseph, from the family of King David.

The young woman's name was Mary.

[pause]

The angel came to her and said:

> Greetings, you who are highly favored! The Lord is with you. [Luke 1:28]

And Mary was deeply troubled, and wondered what kind of greeting this could be.

But the angel said to her:

> Do not be afraid, Mary, for you have found favor with God. [Luke 1:30]

> Behold, you will conceive and give birth to a son, and you are to give Him the name Jesus. [Luke 1:31]

> He will be great and will be called the Son of the Most High. The Lord God will give Him the throne of His father David, and He will reign over the house of Jacob forever. His kingdom will never end! [Luke 1:32–33]

[pause]

Mary asked a simple, honest question. How can this be, since I am a virgin?

And the angel answered:

> The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you. So the Holy One to be born will be called the Son of God. [Luke 1:35]

Then he told her about Elizabeth, her relative, who had been called barren and was now six months along. And he said:

> For no word from God will ever fail. [Luke 1:37]

[long pause]

Think of her in that moment. Young. Engaged to be married. Living in an ordinary town. And now carrying a promise that would change her whole life, and would be very hard to explain to anyone.

And Mary said:

> I am the Lord's servant… May it happen to me according to your word. [Luke 1:38]

And the angel left her.

[long pause]

## Part three: Joseph's dream

Matthew's Gospel tells us what happened next for Joseph. When he learned that Mary was expecting a child, he was a righteous man, and he didn't want to shame her in public. So he decided to end the engagement quietly.

But while he was thinking it over, an angel of the Lord came to him in a dream:

> Joseph, son of David, do not be afraid to embrace Mary as your wife, for the One conceived in her is from the Holy Spirit. [Matthew 1:20]

When Joseph woke up, he did exactly as the angel had told him, and took Mary home as his wife.

[pause]

## Part four: two women in the hill country

Soon after the angel's visit, Mary got ready and hurried to the hill country of Judah, to the home of Zechariah and Elizabeth.

And as soon as she came in and greeted Elizabeth, the baby inside Elizabeth leaped. Elizabeth was filled with the Holy Spirit, and she cried out:

> Blessed are you among women, and blessed is the fruit of your womb! [Luke 1:42]

> And why am I so honored, that the mother of my Lord should come to me? [Luke 1:43]

> Blessed is she who has believed that the Lord's word to her will be fulfilled. [Luke 1:45]

[pause]

Picture these two women together. One old, one young. Both carrying children no one could have expected. Both carrying a promise. Mary had come a long way, perhaps unsure who would believe her. And the first thing she heard was a blessing.

[pause]

And then Mary sang.

> My soul magnifies the Lord, and my spirit rejoices in God my Savior! [Luke 1:46–47]

> For He has looked with favor on the humble state of His servant. From now on all generations will call me blessed. [Luke 1:48]

> For the Mighty One has done great things for me. Holy is His name. [Luke 1:49]

She sang that God's mercy reaches every generation. That He brings down the proud and lifts up the humble. That He fills the hungry with good things. Her song sounds very much like the song Hannah sang, a thousand years before, when she gave her son back to God.

Mary stayed with Elizabeth about three months, and then went home.

[pause]

When Elizabeth's baby was born, the neighbours wanted to call him Zechariah, after his father. But Elizabeth said, “No! He shall be called John.” [Luke 1:60] Zechariah, still unable to speak, asked for a tablet and wrote, “His name is John.” [Luke 1:63] And at that moment his voice came back, and the first thing he did was praise God.

After months of silence, the old priest sang. And his song ends with some of the gentlest words in the whole Bible, about the coming of the Lord his son would prepare the way for:

> because of the tender mercy of our God, by which the Dawn will visit us from on high, [Luke 1:78]

> to shine on those who live in darkness and in the shadow of death, to guide our feet into the path of peace. [Luke 1:79]

The Dawn, visiting from on high. Light for people sitting in the dark. And it was already on its way, growing quietly, in Mary.

[long pause]

## Part five: Bethlehem

Some months later, a decree went out from the emperor, Caesar Augustus, that everyone in the empire must be registered. Everyone went to his own town to register. And so Joseph went up from Nazareth to Bethlehem, the town of David, because he was from David's family. He went with Mary, who was expecting her child.

While they were there, the time came for the baby to be born.

> And she gave birth to her firstborn, a Son. She wrapped Him in swaddling cloths and laid Him in a manger, because there was no room for them in the inn. [Luke 2:7]

[long pause]

A manger. A feeding trough for animals. The Son of the Most High, laid where the animals ate, because there was nowhere else.

[pause]

That same night, out in the fields nearby, shepherds were keeping watch over their flocks. An angel of the Lord appeared to them, and the glory of the Lord shone around them, and they were terrified.

But the angel said:

> Do not be afraid! For behold, I bring you good news of great joy that will be for all the people: [Luke 2:10]

> Today in the city of David a Savior has been born to you. He is Christ the Lord! [Luke 2:11]

And suddenly there were angels beyond counting, praising God:

> Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace to men on whom His favor rests! [Luke 2:14]

[pause]

The shepherds hurried into Bethlehem, and found Mary and Joseph, and the baby lying in the manger, just as they had been told. They told everyone what the angels had said about this child, and all who heard it were amazed.

> But Mary treasured up all these things and pondered them in her heart. [Luke 2:19]

[long pause]

## Part six: in the temple

When the baby was eight days old, He was named Jesus, the name the angel had given before He was born. And when the time came, Mary and Joseph brought Him to the temple in Jerusalem, to present Him to the Lord. They brought the offering the law allowed for families of modest means: a pair of doves, or two young pigeons.

In the temple there was an old man named Simeon, who had been waiting all his life for God's promised rescue. God had shown him that he would not die before he had seen the Lord's Christ. And when he saw the baby, Simeon took Him in his arms and praised God:

> Sovereign Lord, as You have promised, You now dismiss Your servant in peace. [Luke 2:29]

> For my eyes have seen Your salvation [Luke 2:30]

Mary and Joseph were amazed at what was said about Him.

[pause]

Simeon blessed them, and then he said something to Mary alone. That her child would be a sign that many would speak against. And that a sword would pierce her own soul too.

She carried those words with her for many years.

[pause]

There was an old widow in the temple that day too, a prophetess named Anna, eighty-four years old, who worshipped there night and day. She came up at that very moment, and gave thanks to God, and spoke about the child to everyone waiting for God's rescue.

Then Mary and Joseph went home to Nazareth. And the child grew and became strong, filled with wisdom, and the grace of God was on Him.

[long pause]

## Part seven: three days in Jerusalem

Every year, Mary and Joseph went up to Jerusalem for the Feast of the Passover, travelling with relatives and friends. And when Jesus was twelve years old, they went up as usual.

When the feast was over, they set off for home in a large group. They assumed Jesus was somewhere among the relatives and friends, and they travelled a whole day before they began looking for Him. He wasn't there.

[pause]

Any parent knows that feeling. They went back to Jerusalem to search for Him. And after three days, they found Him in the temple courts, sitting among the teachers, listening to them and asking them questions. Everyone who heard Him was amazed at His understanding.

Mary said to Him:

> Child, why have You done this to us? [Luke 2:48]

> Your father and I have been anxiously searching for You. [Luke 2:48]

And Jesus answered:

> Why were you looking for Me? [Luke 2:49]

> Did you not know that I had to be in My Father's house? [Luke 2:49]

They didn't understand what He meant. But He went home with them to Nazareth, and He was obedient to them.

> But His mother treasured up all these things in her heart. [Luke 2:51]

[pause]

There it is again. The second time Luke tells us. Mary didn't understand everything. But she kept it all, and treasured it, and went on raising her son.

> And Jesus grew in wisdom and stature, and in favor with God and man. [Luke 2:52]

[long pause]

## What Scripture remembers

We see Mary only a few more times in the Gospels. At a wedding in Cana, she says to the servants, about her son, “Do whatever He tells you.” [John 2:5] Years later, she is there at the very end, standing near the cross. And Jesus, even then, makes sure she is cared for, giving her into the home of His beloved disciple.

And after He rose, we find her one last time, among the friends of Jesus in Jerusalem, praying together, waiting for what God would do next.

[pause]

## Closing

Mary was not told everything. She was given a promise, and a few words about a sword, and then years of ordinary days: a child to raise, a workshop in a small town, a family to feed. She didn't understand it all. But she treasured it, and pondered it, and kept trusting.

[pause]

Maybe tonight there are things in your life you don't understand yet. You don't need to. You can do what Mary did. Keep them in your heart, turn them over gently, and trust that no word from God will ever fail.

[long pause]

Let your breathing slow now.

Picture the hills outside Bethlehem at night. The shepherds' fires burning low. The sheep asleep. And in the little town below, a mother watching her newborn son sleep, and holding all of it in her heart.

[pause]

Peace on earth, to those on whom His favor rests.

Rest now.

Goodnight.

[long pause]

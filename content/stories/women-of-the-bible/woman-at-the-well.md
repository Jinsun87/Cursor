## Settling in

Let yourself be still now.

Breathe in slowly, and feel the air fill your chest. And let it go, slowly, all the way out.

Let your hands open. Let your face soften. You can stop holding things together now.

[pause]

Tonight's story is a conversation. Just two people talking by a well, in the heat of the day. One of them is Jesus. The other is a woman whose name we're never told. And by the time the conversation ends, her whole town will come running out to meet Him.

You don't need to remember anything. Just listen.

[pause]

## Part one: a tired traveller

Jesus had been in Judea, in the south, where more and more people were coming to be baptized by His disciples. So many, in fact, that the religious leaders were beginning to take notice. And so Jesus left Judea, and set out north for Galilee, with His disciples. And the Bible says He had to pass through Samaria.

Samaria lay between the two, and many Jews travelling that way would go around it if they could. For hundreds of years, Jews and Samaritans had kept apart. They disagreed about their history, and about where God should be worshipped. They did not eat together, or share a cup.

[pause]

Jesus came to a Samaritan town called Sychar, near the field the patriarch Jacob had given to his son Joseph, long ago. Jacob's well was there, a deep well dug in Jacob's time and still in use.

And the Bible tells us something simple and very human:

> Jesus, weary from His journey, sat down by the well. [John 4:6]

It was about the sixth hour, around the middle of the day, by the usual reckoning. His disciples had gone into the town to buy food. And Jesus sat alone by the well, tired and thirsty, in the heat.

[pause]

Hold on to that picture for a moment. The Son of God, worn out from walking, sitting on the edge of a well, waiting.

[long pause]

## Part two: give me a drink

A woman of Samaria came out to draw water.

And Jesus said to her, “Give Me a drink.” [John 4:7]

She was astonished. She said:

> You are a Jew… How can You ask for a drink from me, a Samaritan woman? [John 4:9]

John explains, for readers who might not know, that Jews did not associate with Samaritans. A Jewish teacher, speaking to a Samaritan, and a woman, alone, and asking to drink from her jar. Everything about this conversation was unexpected.

[pause]

Jesus answered her:

> If you knew the gift of God and who is asking you for a drink, you would have asked Him, and He would have given you living water. [John 4:10]

She looked at Him, sitting there with no rope and no bucket. Sir, she said, You have nothing to draw with, and the well is deep. Where would You get this living water? Are You greater than our father Jacob, who gave us this well?

[pause]

And Jesus said:

> Everyone who drinks this water will be thirsty again. [John 4:13]

> But whoever drinks the water I give him will never thirst. Indeed, the water I give him will become in him a fount of water springing up to eternal life. [John 4:14]

[long pause]

Think about that. She came to the well every day. Every day the same walk, the same heavy jar, the same rope. And every day she would be thirsty again.

He was offering her something that wouldn't run out.

[pause]

She said, “Sir, give me this water so that I will not get thirsty and have to keep coming here to draw water.” [John 4:15]

She didn't fully understand yet. But she was asking.

[long pause]

## Part three: everything I ever did

Then Jesus said something unexpected. “Go, call your husband and come back.” [John 4:16]

“I have no husband,” [John 4:17] she said.

And Jesus answered her gently, and plainly. He said she was right. She had had five husbands, and the man she had now was not her husband. “You have spoken truthfully,” [John 4:18] He said.

[pause]

The Bible doesn't tell us her story. Some husbands may have died. Some may have sent her away; in those days a woman could not easily leave a marriage, but a man could. We don't know, and we shouldn't guess too much. What we do know is that Jesus saw her whole life, all of it, and He didn't turn away. He kept talking with her.

[pause]

She said, “Sir… I see that You are a prophet.” [John 4:19]

And then she asked Him about the old argument between their peoples. Our fathers worshipped on this mountain, she said, nodding perhaps toward Mount Gerizim, which rose above the town. But you Jews say the place to worship is Jerusalem.

[pause]

Jesus told her the time was coming when the place wouldn't matter:

> But a time is coming and has now come when the true worshipers will worship the Father in spirit and in truth, for the Father is seeking such as these to worship Him. [John 4:23]

> God is Spirit, and His worshipers must worship Him in spirit and in truth. [John 4:24]

[pause]

The Father is seeking. Not waiting at a temple door for the right people to arrive. Seeking. Out to a Samaritan well, at midday, to a woman nobody else would have stopped to talk to.

[long pause]

## Part four: I am He

The woman said, “I know that Messiah… is coming. When He comes, He will explain everything to us.” [John 4:25]

And Jesus answered her:

> I who speak to you am He. [John 4:26]

[long pause]

It's one of the plainest things Jesus ever said about Himself. And He said it first, not to a priest, or a king, or a crowd, but to her.

[pause]

Just then His disciples came back with the food, and they were surprised to find Him talking with a woman. But none of them asked her what she wanted, or asked Him why He was talking to her.

[pause]

And the woman left her water jar by the well. She forgot all about it. The thing she had come for didn't matter any more.

Picture her hurrying back along the path into Sychar, into the narrow streets where everyone knew her, and knew her story. The very people she might have avoided on the way out. And she went straight to them, and said:

> Come, see a man who told me everything I ever did. Could this be the Christ? [John 4:29]

And they came out of the town, and made their way toward Him.

[long pause]

## Part five: fields ready for harvest

Meanwhile the disciples were urging Jesus to eat something. They had walked all the way into town and back to fetch food. “Rabbi, eat something,” [John 4:31] they said.

But He said:

> I have food to eat that you know nothing about. [John 4:32]

The disciples looked at one another. “Could someone have brought Him food?” [John 4:33] they asked. You can almost hear them, puzzled, looking around the empty well for a basket.

And Jesus explained:

> My food is to do the will of Him who sent Me and to finish His work. [John 4:34]

The tiredness He had sat down with at midday seemed to have gone. Something had fed Him more than bread could: one conversation, one person found.

Then He looked up, perhaps at the fields around the town, and perhaps at the people already coming across them toward the well. And He said to His disciples:

> lift up your eyes and look at the fields, for they are ripe for harvest. [John 4:35]

The fields around Sychar were green, still months from harvest. But the harvest He meant was already walking toward them across those fields: a crowd of Samaritans, coming to see the man the woman had told them about.

> so that the sower and the reaper may rejoice together. [John 4:36]

[pause]

Many of the Samaritans from that town believed in Jesus because of what the woman had told them. He told me everything I ever did.

They asked Him to stay with them. And He did. He stayed there two days. And many more believed because of what He said.

Then they said to the woman:

> We now believe not only because of your words; we have heard for ourselves, and we know that this man truly is the Savior of the world. [John 4:42]

[long pause]

## Closing

She came to the well that day to get water, the way she did every day. And she went home without her jar, but with something far better.

She came as an outsider, someone others might have avoided. Jesus spoke to her anyway, knew everything about her, and offered her living water. And the first person in that town to hear who He was became the one who brought everyone else to Him.

[pause]

Maybe tonight you feel like an outsider, or like someone who has too much history for God to want to talk to. This story says otherwise. The Father is seeking. He knows everything you ever did. And He still sits down by the well and waits for you.

[long pause]

Let your breathing slow now.

Picture the well at Sychar in the cool of the evening. The town quiet. The water deep and still. A forgotten jar resting on the stones.

[pause]

Whoever drinks the water He gives will never thirst.

Rest now.

Goodnight.

[long pause]

## Settling in

Get cosy now, and let your pillow catch your head.

Breathe in slowly, all the way down to your tummy. And let it out, nice and slow.

Wiggle your toes, and then let them be still.

[pause]

Tonight's story is about a boy with a beautiful coat, eleven brothers, some very strange dreams, and a long, long journey that ended in a hug.

Close your eyes. Here we go.

[pause]

## Part one: the coat of many colours

A long time ago, in the land of Canaan, there lived a man named Jacob. Jacob had twelve sons. And of all his sons, the one he loved most was Joseph, because Joseph was born when Jacob was already old.

When Joseph was seventeen, his father gave him a special present.

> so he made him a robe of many colors. [Genesis 37:3]

Imagine it. Red and blue and gold and green, bright in the sunshine, swishing as Joseph walked. None of his brothers had a coat like that.

[pause]

And that was the trouble. When the brothers saw that their father loved Joseph more than all of them, they grew jealous. So jealous that they couldn't say one kind word to him.

[pause]

## Part two: Joseph's dreams

Then Joseph had a dream. And he told his brothers all about it.

> We were binding sheaves of grain in the field, and suddenly my sheaf rose and stood upright, while your sheaves gathered around and bowed down to mine. [Genesis 37:7]

His brothers were cross. “Do you intend to reign over us?” [Genesis 37:8] they said.

Then Joseph had another dream. This time the sun, and the moon, and eleven stars were all bowing down to him. Even his father told him off for that one. But Jacob kept wondering what it might mean.

[pause]

Joseph's brothers didn't wonder. They just grew more and more jealous.

[long pause]

## Part three: down into the pit

One day, Joseph's brothers were far away, looking after the sheep. And Jacob sent Joseph to see how they were.

When the brothers saw him coming in the distance, in his bright coat, they said, “Here comes that dreamer!” [Genesis 37:19]

They were so angry that they wanted to get rid of him for good. But Reuben, the oldest brother, said no. Don't hurt him. Just put him in that pit. Reuben secretly planned to come back and rescue him later.

[pause]

So when Joseph arrived, they pulled off his beautiful coat, and they put him down into a deep pit in the ground. It was dry, with no water in it. And Joseph was alone at the bottom, looking up at the sky.

[pause]

Then some traders came by, with camels carrying spices on their way to Egypt. And another brother, Judah, said, let's not hurt him. He is our brother. Let's sell him instead.

So they pulled Joseph out of the pit, and sold him to the traders for twenty pieces of silver. And the traders took Joseph far away, to Egypt.

[pause]

The brothers took Joseph's coat back to their father, and let him think a wild animal had taken Joseph. And Jacob was so sad that no one could comfort him.

It was a very sad day. But it wasn't the end of the story.

[long pause]

## Part four: the LORD was with Joseph

In Egypt, Joseph was sold as a servant to a man named Potiphar, an important officer of the king. And the Bible says something wonderful:

> And the LORD was with Joseph, and he became a successful man [Genesis 39:2]

Joseph worked hard and did everything well. Soon Potiphar put him in charge of his whole house.

But then someone told a lie about Joseph. Potiphar believed it, and Joseph was put in prison, even though he had done nothing wrong.

[pause]

Even in prison, God didn't forget him.

> the LORD was with him and extended kindness to him [Genesis 39:21]

Before long, the prison keeper put Joseph in charge of all the other prisoners.

[pause]

In prison, two of the king's servants had dreams that troubled them, the king's cupbearer and the king's baker. Joseph said, “Don't interpretations belong to God? Tell me your dreams.” [Genesis 40:8] And God helped Joseph to understand them. The cupbearer was set free, just as Joseph had said.

Joseph asked him to remember him. But the cupbearer forgot all about him. And Joseph waited in prison for two more whole years.

[long pause]

## Part five: the king's dreams

Then one night the king of Egypt, Pharaoh, had two strange dreams.

In the first, seven fat, healthy cows came up out of the river. Then seven thin, bony cows came up and ate them all. In the second, seven good, full heads of grain grew on one stalk, and then seven thin, dry ones swallowed them up.

Nobody in all Egypt could tell Pharaoh what his dreams meant.

And suddenly, the cupbearer remembered. There's a young man in prison, he said, who understands dreams.

[pause]

So Pharaoh sent for Joseph. They hurried him out of prison. He shaved, and put on clean clothes, and stood before the king.

Pharaoh said, I hear you can tell what dreams mean. And Joseph answered:

> I myself cannot do it… but God will give Pharaoh a sound answer. [Genesis 41:16]

[pause]

Then Joseph explained. God was showing Pharaoh what was going to happen. There would be seven years with lots and lots of food. And after that, seven years of famine, when nothing would grow.

So, Joseph said, store up food in the good years, so there will be enough in the hungry years.

[pause]

Pharaoh was amazed. He said, there's no one as wise as you. And he put his own ring on Joseph's finger, dressed him in fine clothes, and put a gold chain around his neck. He made Joseph ruler over all the land of Egypt, second only to the king himself.

Joseph was thirty years old. The boy from the pit was now the most important man in Egypt after Pharaoh.

[long pause]

## Part six: the brothers come

For seven years, there was more food than anyone could eat. And Joseph stored up grain, mountains of it, in every city.

Then the famine came, just as God had said. Nothing grew, anywhere. And people came from every land to buy grain in Egypt.

Back in Canaan, Jacob's family was hungry too. So Jacob sent ten of his sons down to Egypt to buy food. But he kept the youngest, Benjamin, at home with him.

[pause]

The brothers came to Egypt, and they were brought before the governor of the land. And they bowed down to him, with their faces to the ground.

They didn't know who he was. He was dressed like an Egyptian prince. He spoke Egyptian. But Joseph knew them at once.

And he remembered his dream, about the sheaves of grain bowing down.

[pause]

Joseph didn't tell them who he was. Not yet. He tested them, to see if they had changed. And at the end of all the testing, when it seemed Benjamin would have to stay behind as a servant, Judah, the brother who had once sold Joseph, stepped forward. He begged to stay in Benjamin's place instead, so the boy could go home to their father.

The brothers had changed.

[long pause]

## Part seven: I am Joseph

Joseph couldn't hold it in any longer. He sent all his servants out of the room. And then he cried so loudly that people heard it all through the palace.

> I am Joseph! Is my father still alive? [Genesis 45:3]

His brothers were too shocked to speak. So Joseph said, gently:

> Please come near me. [Genesis 45:4]

And he told them not to be upset with themselves.

> because it was to save lives that God sent me before you. [Genesis 45:5]

[pause]

Then Joseph threw his arms around his little brother Benjamin, and they both cried. And he kissed each one of his brothers. And after that, they talked together, for the first time in years.

[pause]

Soon Jacob came down to Egypt too, with the whole family. When Joseph saw his father, he threw his arms around him and wept for a long time. And old Jacob said he was happy at last, because he had seen Joseph's face, and his son was alive.

[long pause]

## Closing

Many years later, Joseph said to his brothers:

> As for you, what you intended against me for evil, God intended for good [Genesis 50:20]

The pit, the prison, the long waiting. It had all been hard. But God was with Joseph the whole time, and in the end He used it all to save many, many people, even the brothers who had hurt him.

[pause]

Sometimes hard things happen, and we can't see why. But God is with us in them, just as He was with Joseph. And He can turn even sad things into something good.

[long pause]

Now let your breathing slow right down.

Picture the land of Egypt at night. The great storehouses full of grain. The river flowing quietly. And somewhere, in a house by the river, a whole family together again, fast asleep.

[pause]

God is with you too, tonight.

Goodnight.

[long pause]

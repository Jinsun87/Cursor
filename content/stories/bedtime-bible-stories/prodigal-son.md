## Settling in

Get comfortable now, and let your whole body relax.

Breathe in slowly, all the way. And let it go, slowly, all the way out.

Once more. In. And out.

[pause]

Tonight's story is one that Jesus told. It's about a boy who ran away from home, a field full of pigs, and a father who never stopped watching the road.

Close your eyes, and listen.

[pause]

## Part one: why Jesus told the story

One day, all sorts of people were crowding round to listen to Jesus. Among them were people that others looked down on. People who had made a mess of their lives. People everyone called sinners.

And some of the religious leaders grumbled. They said:

> This man welcomes sinners and eats with them. [Luke 15:2]

So Jesus told them some stories, to show them what God is like.

[pause]

First, a story about a shepherd with a hundred sheep. One evening he counted them, and there were only ninety-nine. One little sheep was lost, somewhere out in the hills.

So the shepherd left the ninety-nine, and went out to look for the one that was lost. Over the rocks and through the bushes, calling and calling, until at last he found it. And he lifted it up onto his shoulders, and carried it all the way home, rejoicing. And he called his friends and neighbours together, and said, be happy with me! I've found my lost sheep!

Jesus said that in the same way, there is more joy in heaven over one person who turns back to God, than over ninety-nine who never went away.

[pause]

Then a story about a woman with ten silver coins, who lost just one. She lit a lamp, and swept the whole house, looking in every corner, under every chair, until at last, there it was, shining in the dust. And she called her friends to celebrate.

> there is joy in the presence of God's angels over one sinner who repents. [Luke 15:10]

And then, the most famous story of all.

[long pause]

## Part two: two sons

There was once a man who had two sons.

The younger son said to his father, give me my share of everything that will be mine one day. Give it to me now.

And the father did. He divided everything he had between his two sons.

[pause]

A few days later, the younger son packed up all his things, and set off on a long journey to a faraway country. And there, he spent all his money. He wasted it, on silly, selfish things, having a wild time, until every last coin was gone.

[long pause]

## Part three: the pigs

Just when his money ran out, a famine came to that country. There was hardly any food anywhere. And the young man was hungry.

So he found a job, with a farmer in that country. And the farmer sent him out into the fields, to look after the pigs.

[pause]

Picture him there. Muddy. Tired. His fine clothes all torn and dirty. The pigs snorting and snuffling around his feet. And he was so hungry that he wished he could eat the pods the pigs were eating. But nobody gave him anything.

[long pause]

Then, one day, sitting in the mud with the pigs, the young man came to his senses. He thought:

> How many of my father's hired servants have plenty of food, but here I am, starving to death! [Luke 15:17]

So he decided. I'll go home. I'll say to my father:

> Father, I have sinned against heaven and against you. [Luke 15:18]

> I am no longer worthy to be called your son. Make me like one of your hired servants. [Luke 15:19]

And he got up, and started the long walk home.

[long pause]

## Part four: the father runs

Now all this time, his father had been waiting. Watching the road. Every day.

And one day, far, far away, at the very end of the road, the father saw a small, thin, ragged figure, walking slowly toward home.

[pause]

> But while he was still in the distance, his father saw him and was filled with compassion. He ran to his son, embraced him, and kissed him. [Luke 15:20]

[long pause]

He ran! In those days, a grown-up, important man would never run. But this father picked up his robe, and ran down the road, as fast as he could. And he threw his arms around his son, all muddy and smelly as he was, and hugged him, and kissed him.

[pause]

The son started his speech. Father, I have sinned against heaven and against you. I'm not good enough to be called your son any more...

But the father didn't even let him finish. He called to his servants:

> Quick! Bring the best robe and put it on him. Put a ring on his finger and sandals on his feet. [Luke 15:22]

And let's have a feast, and celebrate!

> For this son of mine was dead and is alive again! He was lost and is found! [Luke 15:24]

And so they began to celebrate.

[long pause]

## Part five: the older brother

Now the older son had been out working in the fields all day. As he came near the house, he heard music, and dancing.

What's going on? he asked one of the servants. And the servant said, your brother has come home! And your father is having a feast, because he's back safe and sound.

[pause]

But the older brother was angry. He wouldn't go in. He said to his father, I've worked for you all these years, and always done what you said. And you never gave me a party. But when this son of yours comes home, after wasting all your money, you throw a feast for him!

[pause]

And the father said to him, very gently:

> Son, you are always with me… and all that is mine is yours. [Luke 15:31]

> But it was fitting to celebrate and be glad, because this brother of yours was dead and is alive again; he was lost and is found. [Luke 15:32]

[long pause]

And that's where Jesus ended the story. We never find out whether the older brother went in to the party. Maybe Jesus wanted everyone listening to ask themselves: would I go in?

[long pause]

## Closing

Jesus told this story to show us what God is like.

God is like that father. Even when we've gone far away, even when we've made a big mess of things, He's still watching the road, waiting for us. And when we turn around and come home, He doesn't just let us in. He runs to meet us.

[pause]

Whatever you've done today, whatever went wrong, you can always come home to God. He'll be waiting with open arms.

[long pause]

Now let your breathing slow right down.

Picture that house in the evening, after the feast. The music finished. The lamps glowing in the windows. And a young man in a clean robe and new sandals, asleep in his own bed again, safe at home.

[pause]

You're home too. Safe and loved.

Goodnight.

[long pause]

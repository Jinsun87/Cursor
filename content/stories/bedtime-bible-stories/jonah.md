## Settling in

Snuggle down now, under your blanket.

Take a slow breath in. And let it out, like a long, quiet sigh.

Once more. In. And out.

[pause]

Tonight's story has a ship, a storm, a very big fish, and a man who tried to run away from God, and found out that you can't.

Close your eyes, and let's set sail.

[pause]

## Part one: running away

Once there was a man named Jonah. Jonah was a prophet, which means God gave him messages to tell people.

One day God said to Jonah:

> Get up! Go to the great city of Nineveh and preach against it, because its wickedness has come up before Me. [Jonah 1:2]

Nineveh was a huge city, far away to the east. The people there were very wicked. And Jonah didn't want to go. Not one bit.

[pause]

So Jonah got up, and went the opposite way! He went down to the seaside town of Joppa, and found a ship sailing far, far away to the west, to a place called Tarshish. He paid his fare, climbed on board, and sailed off, trying to get away from God.

[long pause]

## Part two: the storm

But you can't run away from God.

Out on the sea, God sent a great wind. The waves grew bigger and bigger, and the ship rocked and creaked so hard it seemed it might break apart.

The sailors were very frightened. They threw their cargo into the sea to make the ship lighter. And each one prayed to his own god.

[pause]

And where was Jonah? Fast asleep, down in the bottom of the ship!

The captain went down and woke him up. How can you sleep? Get up, and pray to your God! Maybe your God will save us!

[pause]

Then the sailors said, someone on this ship has brought this trouble on us. Who is it? And they found out it was Jonah.

So Jonah told them the truth:

> I am a Hebrew… I worship the LORD, the God of the heavens, who made the sea and the dry land. [Jonah 1:9]

And he told them he was running away from God.

[pause]

The storm grew worse and worse. And the sailors asked Jonah, what should we do?

Jonah said, throw me into the sea, and the sea will be calm. It's my fault.

The sailors didn't want to. They rowed as hard as they could to get back to land. But they couldn't. So at last, they prayed to the LORD, and did what Jonah said.

And as soon as Jonah splashed into the water, the wind stopped, and the sea grew calm.

[long pause]

## Part three: the great fish

Down, down, down went Jonah into the deep, dark sea.

But God had a plan.

> Now the LORD had appointed a great fish to swallow Jonah, and Jonah spent three days and three nights in the belly of the fish. [Jonah 1:17]

[pause]

Imagine it. Inside a great big fish! All dark and squishy, and smelling very, very fishy. Seaweed everywhere. The fish swimming along through the deep blue water, with Jonah inside.

And there, in the belly of the fish, Jonah did something he should have done at the start. He prayed.

> In my distress I called to the LORD, and He answered me. [Jonah 2:2]

He thanked God for saving him. And he said:

> Salvation is from the LORD! [Jonah 2:9]

[pause]

Then God spoke to the fish. And the fish swam up to the shore, and with a great big whoosh, spat Jonah out onto the dry land.

Splat! There he was, on the sand, blinking in the sunshine. Probably a bit slimy. But safe.

[long pause]

## Part four: Nineveh

Then God spoke to Jonah a second time:

> Get up! Go to the great city of Nineveh and proclaim to it the message that I give you. [Jonah 3:2]

And this time, Jonah went.

[pause]

Nineveh was so big, it took three days to walk across it. Jonah walked into the city, and called out God's message. In forty days, Nineveh will be overturned!

And a surprising thing happened. The people of Nineveh listened. They believed God. They were sorry for all the wicked things they had done. Everyone, from the most important to the least, stopped eating and wore rough sackcloth, to show how sorry they were.

Even the king got up from his throne, took off his royal robe, and sat down in the dust. He told everyone to pray to God, and to stop doing wicked things.

[pause]

And when God saw that they were truly sorry, and had turned away from their wicked ways, He was kind to them. He didn't punish the city after all.

[long pause]

## Part five: Jonah's shady plant

You might think Jonah would be happy. But he wasn't. He was cross!

Because Jonah had known all along that God would be kind to Nineveh. That's why he ran away. He said to God:

> I knew that You are a gracious and compassionate God, slow to anger, abounding in loving devotion [Jonah 4:2]

Jonah didn't want God to be kind to them. So he went out of the city, and sat down in a grumpy heap, in the hot sun, to see what would happen.

[pause]

So God made a plant grow up beside Jonah, a big leafy vine, to shade him from the sun. And Jonah was very happy with his plant.

But the next morning, God sent a little worm, and the worm nibbled the plant, and the plant withered away. Then the hot sun beat down, and a hot wind blew, and Jonah was miserable. And he was angry about his plant.

[pause]

So God said to Jonah, you cared about that plant, though you didn't plant it or make it grow. It came up in a night, and it was gone in a night.

> So should I not care about the great city of Nineveh, which has more than 120,000 people who cannot tell their right hand from their left, and many cattle as well? [Jonah 4:11]

[long pause]

And that's how the book of Jonah ends. With God's question, still hanging in the air.

[long pause]

## Closing

Jonah ran away from God. But God didn't give up on Jonah. He went after him, and sent a fish to save him, and gave him a second chance.

And God didn't give up on Nineveh either. He loved all those people, and even all their cows.

[pause]

God is gracious and kind, slow to get angry, and full of love. That's what made Jonah cross. But it's good news for you and me. Because God never gives up on us either.

[long pause]

Now let your breathing slow right down.

Picture the sea at night, calm and still. A great fish gliding slowly through the dark blue water, far below. And up on the hill, the great city of Nineveh, all its lamps going out, one by one, as everyone goes to sleep.

[pause]

God cares about you, even more than Jonah cared about his plant.

Goodnight.

[long pause]

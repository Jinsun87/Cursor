## Settling in

Lie back now, and let yourself feel heavy and warm.

Take a slow breath in through your nose. And let it whoosh out, slowly, like the wind.

Once more. In. And out.

[pause]

Tonight's story begins with a tiny baby in a basket on a river, and ends with a whole people walking through the middle of the sea. It's the story of Moses.

Close your eyes, and listen.

[pause]

## Part one: a baby in a basket

Many years after Joseph, his family had grown into a great people, living in Egypt. They were called the Israelites, or the Hebrews.

But a new king came to rule Egypt, a Pharaoh who didn't remember Joseph. He was afraid of the Israelites, because there were so many of them. So he made them slaves. He made them work very hard, making bricks and building cities, all day long under the hot sun.

And then Pharaoh made a cruel rule. Baby boys born to the Hebrews were not safe.

[pause]

Around that time, a Hebrew mother had a baby boy. He was a beautiful baby, and she hid him at home for three whole months. But babies grow, and get louder, and soon she couldn't hide him any more.

So she made a little basket out of papyrus reeds, and painted it with tar so it would float. She laid her baby inside, wrapped up snug. And she set the basket among the tall reeds at the edge of the river Nile.

And the baby's big sister hid nearby, watching, to see what would happen.

[pause]

Picture it. The basket bobbing gently on the water. The reeds whispering in the breeze. The baby asleep inside.

[long pause]

## Part two: drawn out of the water

Then who should come down to the river, but Pharaoh's own daughter, the princess, to bathe. She saw the basket in the reeds, and sent her servant to fetch it.

When she opened it, the baby was crying. And the princess felt sorry for him. She said, “This is one of the Hebrew children.” [Exodus 2:6]

[pause]

Quick as a flash, the baby's sister ran up. Shall I go and find one of the Hebrew women to feed the baby for you?

“Go ahead,” [Exodus 2:8] said the princess.

And who do you think the sister fetched? The baby's very own mother!

So the princess said to the mother, take this baby and look after him for me, and I'll pay you. And the mother took her own little boy home again, safe.

[pause]

When the boy was older, his mother brought him to the princess, and he became her son. She named him Moses, because, she said, “I drew him out of the water.” [Exodus 2:10]

[long pause]

## Part three: the burning bush

Moses grew up in Pharaoh's palace. But he never forgot that he was a Hebrew. When he was a grown man, he had to run away from Egypt, far into the land of Midian. There he became a shepherd, looking after sheep in the wilderness.

One day, leading the sheep near a mountain, Moses saw something strange. A bush was on fire. But it wasn't burning up.

Moses went closer to look. And God called to him from the bush:

> Moses, Moses! [Exodus 3:4]

“Here I am,” [Exodus 3:4] said Moses.

[pause]

God told Moses that He had seen how His people were suffering in Egypt. He had heard them crying. And He was going to set them free. And God said:

> Therefore, go! I am sending you to Pharaoh to bring My people the Israelites out of Egypt. [Exodus 3:10]

[long pause]

## Part four: let my people go

So Moses went back to Egypt, with his brother Aaron. They stood before Pharaoh, and said:

> This is what the LORD, the God of Israel, says: Let My people go [Exodus 5:1]

But Pharaoh said no.

So God sent sign after sign. Ten of them, one after another, each harder than the last. And every time, Pharaoh said no.

Until at last, after the tenth, Pharaoh called for Moses in the middle of the night, and said, go! Take your people and go!

[pause]

And so they went. A great crowd of people, with their children and their animals and everything they could carry, walking out of Egypt, free.

And God went with them. In the daytime, He went ahead of them in a tall pillar of cloud, to show them the way. And at night, in a pillar of fire, to give them light.

> Neither the pillar of cloud by day nor the pillar of fire by night left its place before the people. [Exodus 13:22]

[long pause]

## Part five: trapped by the sea

The people walked until they came to the edge of the sea, and there they made camp.

But back in Egypt, Pharaoh changed his mind. Why did we let them go? So he got into his chariot, and gathered his army, all his horses and chariots, and chased after them.

When the Israelites looked up and saw the army coming, they were terrified. The sea was in front of them. The army was behind them. There was nowhere to go.

[pause]

But Moses said to the people:

> Do not be afraid. Stand firm and you will see the LORD's salvation, which He will accomplish for you today; [Exodus 14:13]

> The LORD will fight for you; you need only to be still. [Exodus 14:14]

[long pause]

You need only to be still. Just like you, now, lying still in your bed.

[pause]

Then the pillar of cloud moved. It went from the front of the people, to stand behind them, between the Israelites and the army. On the Egyptians' side it was dark. On the Israelites' side it lit up the night. And all night long, the army couldn't come near.

[long pause]

## Part six: through the sea

Then God told Moses to stretch out his hand over the sea.

And all that night, God sent a strong wind from the east. It blew, and blew, and blew. And the sea began to move. The water rolled back, and back, and stood up on either side, like two great walls. And in the middle, the ground was dry.

[pause]

> and the Israelites went through the sea on dry ground, with walls of water on their right and on their left. [Exodus 14:22]

Imagine walking down there, in the middle of the night. The fire of the pillar glowing behind you. The walls of water rising high on either side, shining. Fish swimming past, perhaps, just behind the glassy water. Your feet on the dry sand. All the families walking together, the children holding their parents' hands. All the way across.

[long pause]

The army tried to follow them in. But their chariot wheels got stuck, and they couldn't drive. And when every last Israelite had reached the other side, Moses stretched out his hand again. As morning came, the waters flowed back together, and Pharaoh's army was gone. They never chased the Israelites again.

[pause]

That day, God saved His people. And they trusted in the LORD, and in His servant Moses.

[long pause]

## Part seven: a song by the sea

On the far shore, Moses' sister Miriam picked up her tambourine. And all the women joined her, with tambourines and dancing, singing:

> Sing to the LORD, for He is highly exalted [Exodus 15:21]

Can you hear it? The tambourines, the singing, the laughter, carried on the morning wind over the quiet sea.

[long pause]

## Closing

The baby in the basket grew up to lead God's people out of Egypt, and through the sea. God took care of Moses when he was tiny and floating on the river. And God took care of all His people when they were trapped by the sea.

When they were frightened, God told them to be still, and watch Him take care of them.

[pause]

You can be still too, tonight. You don't have to fight your worries. The LORD will fight for you. You only need to be still.

[long pause]

Now let your breathing slow down.

Picture the sea at night, calm and quiet again. The stars shining on the water. And on the shore, all the families camped together, safe, with the pillar of fire glowing softly over them all night long.

[pause]

Be still, and sleep.

Goodnight.

[long pause]

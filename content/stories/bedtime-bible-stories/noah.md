## Settling in

Snuggle down now, and get comfortable.

Take a big, slow breath in, as if you're smelling a flower. And let it out slowly, as if you're blowing out a candle.

One more time. In. And out.

[pause]

Tonight's story is about a very big boat, a great many animals, a long, long rain, and a promise you can still see in the sky today.

Close your eyes, and let's begin.

[pause]

## Part one: a good man

A long, long time ago, near the very beginning of the world, there lived a man named Noah.

In those days, people had stopped caring about what was right. They were unkind and cruel to each other, all the time. And when God looked at the world He had made, the Bible says He was grieved in His heart. God was sad.

But Noah was different.

> Noah, however, found favor in the eyes of the LORD. [Genesis 6:8]

The Bible says Noah walked with God. He listened to God, and he did what was right, even when no one else did.

Noah had a wife, and three grown-up sons. Their names were Shem, and Ham, and Japheth. And each of the sons had a wife too.

[pause]

## Part two: build a boat

One day God spoke to Noah. He told Noah that a great flood was coming, so much water that it would cover the whole earth.

But God had a plan to keep Noah and his family safe. He said:

> Make for yourself an ark of gopher wood; make rooms in the ark and coat it with pitch inside and out. [Genesis 6:14]

An ark is a big boat. And this ark was going to be very big indeed. God told Noah exactly how long, how wide and how tall to make it. It was longer than a football field, taller than a house, with three decks, one on top of the other, a roof over the top, and a big door in the side.

[pause]

Imagine Noah and his sons, day after day, cutting the wood. The sound of hammers and saws. The smell of sawdust and of sticky black pitch, painted on the inside and the outside, so that not one drop of water could get in.

It took a long time. But the Bible says:

> So Noah did everything precisely as God had commanded him. [Genesis 6:22]

[long pause]

## Part three: the animals come

When the ark was finished, God told Noah to bring the animals in. Two of every kind of creature, a male and a female. And seven pairs of some of them, and of the birds too, so that there would be plenty of every kind when the flood was over.

And so they came.

[pause]

Picture them coming, two by two, across the fields toward the ark. Lions, padding softly. Elephants, swinging their trunks. Sheep and goats and cows. Little mice scurrying, and rabbits hopping. Birds of every colour, flying down to land on the roof. Slow tortoises taking their time, and the very last of them all, perhaps, a sleepy snail.

The Bible says they came to Noah to go into the ark, two by two, every creature that had the breath of life.

[pause]

Then Noah went in, with his wife, and his sons, and their wives. And then the Bible says something very gentle:

> Then the LORD shut him in. [Genesis 7:16]

Noah didn't have to close the great door himself. God closed it. God shut them in, safe and sound.

[long pause]

## Part four: the rain

And then it began to rain.

Pitter-patter at first, on the wooden roof. Then harder. And harder. The rain fell for forty days and forty nights.

[pause]

Listen to it, if you like. The rain drumming on the roof above. The sound of it all around. And inside the ark, the animals settling down in their stalls. The cows chewing quietly. The birds tucking their heads under their wings. Noah's family lighting a little lamp.

[pause]

The water rose higher and higher, and the ark began to float. It lifted up off the ground, and floated, gently rocking, on top of the water. Higher than the trees. Higher than the hills. Higher, even, than the tops of the mountains.

Everything outside the ark was gone under the water. That was the sad part, and it made God sad too. But inside the ark, Noah and his family and all the animals were safe, because God had kept them.

[pause]

The ark floated on the waters for days and days, and weeks and weeks. Rocking slowly, like a cradle.

[long pause]

## Part five: God remembered

And then, the Bible says:

> But God remembered Noah and all the animals and livestock that were with him in the ark. [Genesis 8:1]

God sent a wind to blow over the earth. A soft, steady wind. And slowly, very slowly, the water began to go down.

One day there was a bump. The ark had come to rest on top of a mountain, the mountains of Ararat.

And as the water went down further and further, the tops of the other mountains began to peek out, one by one, like islands in the sea.

[long pause]

## Part six: the dove

After forty more days, Noah opened a window in the ark. He sent out a raven, a big black bird, and it flew back and forth over the water.

Then Noah sent out a dove, a soft grey and white dove, to see if the land was dry. But there was still water everywhere. The dove couldn't find anywhere to land. So she flew back to the ark. And Noah reached out his hand, and gently brought her back inside.

[pause]

Noah waited seven days. And then he sent the dove out again.

All day she was gone. And then, in the evening, she came back. And in her beak was something green.

> a freshly plucked olive leaf [Genesis 8:11]

A leaf! Somewhere out there, the trees were growing again. Noah knew the water was going down.

[pause]

Noah waited seven more days, and sent the dove out one more time. And this time, she didn't come back. She had found a place to make her home.

[long pause]

## Part seven: coming out

At last, the ground was dry. They had been in the ark for about a whole year. And God said to Noah:

> Come out of the ark, you and your wife, along with your sons and their wives. [Genesis 8:16]

And so the great door opened, and out they came. Noah and his family, blinking in the sunshine. And all the animals, two by two, stretching their legs, running and hopping and flying out into the fresh new world. The grass was green and wet. The air smelled clean.

[pause]

The very first thing Noah did was build an altar, to thank God for keeping them safe.

And God made a promise:

> As long as the earth endures, seedtime and harvest, cold and heat, summer and winter, day and night shall never cease. [Genesis 8:22]

[long pause]

## Part eight: the rainbow

Then God said He would never again send a flood to cover the whole earth. And He gave a sign, so that everyone would remember the promise.

> I have set My rainbow in the clouds, and it will be a sign of the covenant between Me and the earth. [Genesis 9:13]

And there it was. Stretching right across the sky. Red and orange and yellow and green and blue and violet. The first rainbow.

[pause]

God said that whenever a rainbow appears in the clouds, He will see it, and remember His promise, to Noah and to every living thing.

[long pause]

## Closing

So the next time you see a rainbow after the rain, you can remember Noah. And you can remember that God keeps His promises. Every single one.

[pause]

God kept Noah safe through the storm, inside the ark, with the door shut tight. And God watches over you too, tonight, while the world outside is dark and quiet.

[long pause]

Now let your breathing slow down.

Picture the ark resting on the mountain at night, still and peaceful. The animals all asleep inside. The rain finished. The stars coming out over the clean, new world.

[pause]

Day and night shall never cease. And now it's night time. Time to rest.

Goodnight.

[long pause]

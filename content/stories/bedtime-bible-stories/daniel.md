## Settling in

Settle down now, and let yourself get warm and sleepy.

Breathe in slowly. And breathe out, slowly. Feel your tummy rise and fall.

In. And out.

[pause]

Tonight's story is about a man who prayed every day, a king who couldn't sleep, and a den full of lions who, for one whole night, didn't open their mouths.

Close your eyes. Let's begin.

[pause]

## Part one: Daniel far from home

A long time ago, when Daniel was just a young man, an army came from the great city of Babylon and took him far away from his home in Jerusalem. He was taken to Babylon, with some of his friends, to learn and to work in the king's palace.

Daniel was very far from home. But he never forgot God. Wherever he was, he kept praying, and he kept doing what was right.

Right at the start, the king's servants wanted Daniel and his friends to eat the king's own rich food and drink his wine. But Daniel had decided to stay faithful to God, even in that. So he politely asked: please, just let us have vegetables to eat and water to drink, for ten days, and then see how we look.

And at the end of the ten days, Daniel and his friends looked healthier and stronger than all the other young men who ate the king's food. And God gave them wisdom, and helped them to learn and understand all kinds of things.

[pause]

Years and years went by. Kings came and went. And by the time of our story, Daniel was an old man, with a white beard, and a new king was on the throne. His name was Darius.

[long pause]

## Part two: the best helper

King Darius had a very big kingdom. So he chose a hundred and twenty men to help him rule it. And over them all, he put three leaders. One of the three was Daniel.

And Daniel did his work so well, so wisely and so honestly, that the king planned to put him in charge of the whole kingdom.

[pause]

But the other leaders didn't like that one bit. They were jealous. So they watched Daniel very carefully, looking for anything he did wrong.

They watched, and they watched. But they couldn't find a single thing. Daniel was honest. He worked hard. He never cheated, and he never told lies.

[pause]

So the men said to each other:

> We will never find any charge against this Daniel unless we find something against him concerning the law of his God. [Daniel 6:5]

And they came up with a sneaky plan.

[long pause]

## Part three: the sneaky law

The men went to King Darius, and bowed, and said, O King, live for ever! We think you should make a new law. For thirty days, nobody may pray to any god or any person, except to you, O king. And anyone who breaks the law will be thrown into the den of lions.

The king liked the idea. It made him feel very important. So he signed the law. And in that kingdom, once the king had signed a law, it could never be changed.

[pause]

Now what do you think Daniel did, when he heard about the new law?

> he went into his house, where the windows of his upper room opened toward Jerusalem, and three times a day he got down on his knees, prayed, and gave thanks to his God, just as he had done before. [Daniel 6:10]

Just as he had done before. Daniel didn't hide. He didn't stop. He just kept on praying, by his open window, three times a day.

[long pause]

## Part four: the king is sad

Of course, the jealous men were watching. They saw Daniel praying, and they hurried to the king.

O king! Didn't you make a law? Daniel doesn't care about your law. He still prays to his God, three times a day!

[pause]

When the king heard this, he was very upset. He liked Daniel. He didn't want anything to happen to him. All day long, until the sun went down, he tried to find a way to save Daniel.

But the men reminded him. The law can't be changed, O king. Not even by you.

[pause]

So, very sadly, the king gave the order. And Daniel was put into the den of lions.

Before they closed it, the king called down to Daniel:

> May your God, whom you serve continually, deliver you! [Daniel 6:16]

Then a big stone was rolled over the mouth of the den, and the king sealed it with his own ring.

[long pause]

## Part five: a long night

The king went back to his palace. But he couldn't eat his supper. He didn't want any music. And he couldn't sleep, not one wink.

> and sleep fled from him. [Daniel 6:18]

All night long the king lay awake, worrying about Daniel.

[pause]

And down in the den?

Picture it. The dark, cool den. And the lions, big and golden, with their great paws and their shaggy manes. And in the middle of them, Daniel.

But the lions didn't roar. They didn't come near him. Their mouths stayed shut, all night long. Maybe they lay down and slept, curled up like big cats. Maybe Daniel even leaned his back against one of them, warm and soft, and slept too.

Because God was there, in the den, with Daniel.

[long pause]

## Part six: in the morning

At the very first light of dawn, the king jumped up, and hurried to the den of lions. And he called out, in a worried voice:

> O Daniel, servant of the living God, has your God, whom you serve continually, been able to deliver you from the lions? [Daniel 6:20]

And from down in the den, came Daniel's voice:

> My God sent His angel and shut the mouths of the lions. They have not hurt me [Daniel 6:22]

[pause]

The king was so happy! He told his servants to lift Daniel out of the den, straight away. And when they pulled him out, there wasn't a single scratch on him. Not one.

Because he had trusted in his God.

[long pause]

## Part seven: the king's letter

Then King Darius wrote a letter to everyone in his whole kingdom, to people of every land and language. He wrote that everyone should honour the God of Daniel, because:

> For He is the living God, and He endures forever; His kingdom will never be destroyed, and His dominion will never end. [Daniel 6:26]

> He delivers and rescues [Daniel 6:27]

And the jealous men were never able to trouble Daniel again. Daniel did well all the rest of his days.

[long pause]

## Closing

Daniel kept on praying, every single day, just as he always had. When it was easy, and when it was hard. Even when it was dangerous.

And God was with him, even in the lions' den.

[pause]

You can talk to God too, any time you like. In the morning, or at bedtime, or in the middle of the night. You don't need a window that faces Jerusalem. You can just close your eyes, and tell Him what's in your heart.

[long pause]

Now let your breathing slow right down.

Picture the lions' den in the soft light of morning. The lions yawning and stretching, sleepy and calm. And Daniel, safe and smiling, being lifted up into the sunshine.

[pause]

God is with you tonight, just as He was with Daniel.

Goodnight.

[long pause]
